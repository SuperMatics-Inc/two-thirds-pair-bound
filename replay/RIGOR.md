# A rare-history expansion for slow phonons on the square lattice

## 1. Statement

**PROVED.** On nearest-neighbor square tori, take two electron spin species, identical independent neutral harmonic oscillators, and no other electron interactions. Use bare hopping `−t`, `t>0`, a site term `b_x n_x` for Holstein, and hopping `−t+b_e` for bond SSH. Here `b=FX`, `U=F²/K>0`, `Ω=ℏ√(K/m)>0`, `τ=k_B T>0`, and `β=1/τ`; `t,U` are held fixed in the adiabatic limit. Set `E=t` for Holstein and `E=t+U` for SSH. If

```
τ³ ≥ 2²⁵ U Ω² [1 + log(1+E/τ)],                         (1)
```

then the equal-time two-point function of any fixed finite-support electronic fields of opposite nonzero even charge decays exponentially: the bound is uniform in grand-canonical volume and real chemical potential and holds in periodic thermodynamic canonical states at every interior limiting density. At fixed parameters its decay rate can be taken to be `a/2`, with `a=(1+16βJ)^(-1)`, `J=t` for Holstein and `J=t+4096(t+U+τ)` for SSH (changes of the support radii only affect the prefactor). In particular `k_B T_pair≤τ_*`, where `T_pair` is the supremum of temperatures with algebraic correlations of a fixed local electronic charge-two field, allowing oscillations and multiplicative logarithms, and `τ_*` solves equality in (1). At fixed `t,U>0`,

```
τ_* ∼ [(2/3) 2²⁵ U]^(1/3) Ω^(2/3) [log(E/Ω)]^(1/3).   (2)
```

This is an asymptotic of the upper bound, not of a physical transition. It concerns conventional BKT only if detected by such an electronic field. No finite-mass helicity bound, whole Yang-matrix bound, phonon-dressed-order bound, or linear comparison with the bare Migdal–Eliashberg scale is asserted.

## 2. Exact histories and the Gaussian large-amplitude estimate

For each oscillator the free-trace-normalized imaginary-time covariance on `[0,β]` is

```
D(u,v) = (U/β) Σ_(k∈Z) [Ω²/(ν_k²+Ω²)] exp(iν_k(u−v)),   ν_k=2πk/β.
```

Indeed the oscillator kinetic operator in `b` is `−(UΩ²/2)∂_b²` and the potential is `b²/(2U)`, giving the periodic inverse of `−∂_u²+Ω²` times `UΩ²`. Equivalently solve the periodic Green function with the jump in its derivative at zero; the solution is `(UΩ/2)(e^(−Ωd)+e^(−Ω(β−d)))/(1−e^(−βΩ))`, `d=|u−v|`. In particular it is pointwise nonnegative and has integral `U` in either time. Independent such histories give the exact partition and electronic insertions by Gaussian Wick in the electronic Dyson series: the oscillator time-ordered moments are Wick moments of `D` (by its creation and annihilation representation), and the electronic time ordering is retained. For finite volume absolute re-summation follows from a Gaussian exponential moment of each `∫|b(u)|du`, by Jensen in time. One can equivalently obtain the representation by time slicing the finite-matrix electronic evolution with the harmonic heat kernel. All oscillator traces below are divided by the full free oscillator traces. The free histories are continuous (the diagonal heat-kernel bridge is a Brownian bridge with a quadratic weight), so their time averages and supremum are well-defined.

Write each history as `b_0+v(u)`, where `b_0` is a scalar Gaussian of variance `U/β` independent of the zero-mean Gaussian history `v`, by orthogonality of the Gaussian Fourier modes. Put `d_0=UβΩ²`. The increment variance of `v` is bounded by `UΩ² |u−w|`. To see this, bound the nonzero Fourier coefficients of `D` by `(UΩ²/β)ν_k^(−2)`, and apply Parseval to the indicator of the interval of length `l=|u−w|`: its nonzero coefficients, with normalization `1/β`, have squared moduli `|exp(iν_k l)−1|²/(β²ν_k²)`. The resulting sum for the variance is at most `UΩ² l(1−l/β)`.

For `S=||v||_∞`, zero time average implies `S≤2 sup_u|v(u)−v(0)|`. A dyadic argument gives

```
P(S>s) ≤ 8 exp(−s²/(64 d_0)),    s²/(64 d_0)≥16.       (3)
```

At dyadic level `k≥1`, connect each new node to its left parent on level `k−1`; the node at the periodic endpoint equals that at zero. There are at most `2^k` new links, length `β 2^(−k)`. Require each linked increment to have modulus at most `s (3/4)^(k−1)/8`. The thresholds sum to `s/2`, so by continuity all required inequalities imply `S≤s`. The centered Gaussian tail `P(|X|>l)≤2 exp(−l²/(2 Var X))` follows by applying the Gaussian moment generating function to both signs. Thus the failure probability at level `k` is at most `2^(k+1) exp[−A(9/8)^(k−1)]`, `A=s²/(64d_0)`. Since `(9/8)^m≥1+m/8`, the sum is at most `8e^(−A)` when `A≥16` (the geometric ratio is at most `2e^(−2)<1/2`).

Use the dynamic cutoff `r=τ/64` and put `g=τ³/(UΩ²)`. For `g≥2²³`, (3) and integration of the tail imply

```
E[1_(S>r) exp(1024β S)] ≤ 16 exp(−g/2¹⁹).             (4)
```

In fact let `Y=β S`, `l=1/64`. Then `P(Y>y)≤8 exp(−g y²/64)` for `y≥l` and `1024 y≤g y²/128`. Integration by parts for the positive tail moment gives `e^(1024 l)P(Y>l)+1024∫_l^∞ e^(1024 y)P(Y>y)dy`. Use `y²≥l²+l(y−l)` to bound it by `8[1+1024·128/(g l)]exp(−g l²/128)`. This proves (4). No interacting phonon probability or global good-history event is used here.

## 3. Bounded time-dependent reference and its Wick bounds

### Lemma 1: Wick control for a bounded, time-dependent quadratic reference

**PROVED.** Let `h` be any real, spin-independent static one-particle matrix on a square torus with hopping bounded by `J`, arbitrary diagonal and real chemical potential. Let `V(u)` be a continuous real perturbation that is either purely diagonal on sites or purely on nearest-neighbor bonds, with each coefficient bounded by `r=1/(64β)`, periodic in time. The normalized electronic reference trace is nonzero. Its Wick covariance has weighted row/column `ℓ²` bound `2`, attachment bound `Q=64(1+4βJ)` as specified below, and every balanced `k×k` Wick minor after a positive-semidefinite, diagonal-one vertex interpolation has modulus at most `4^k`. All bounds are uniform in volume, static diagonal, chemical potential, history, and insertion times. **Proof:** the rest of this subsection proves these assertions, including the Wick representation and its interpolated minor bound. Elementary constants used below require no imported numerical calculation: `2<e<3` follows from `e=Σ_n1/n!` and `n!≥2^(n−1)` with strict inequality for `n≥3`; `π>2` follows from `π=4∫_0^1(1+x²)^(-1)dx>2`.

Fix arbitrary static site coordinates in Holstein. In SSH keep reference static coordinates `|b_0|≤R`, where `R=4096(t+U+τ)`. For both models keep each reference history `||v||_∞≤r`. Denote the resulting real one-particle Hamiltonian, including chemical potential, by `h+V(u)` per spin, where `h` is static with hopping bounded by `J` as in §1; `V(u)` is the diagonal site potential or bond-hopping perturbation. We establish both spatial summability and a determinant bound for its normalized time-ordered Wick functional. These bounds apply pointwise to the bounded reference, not to an arbitrary full history.

Let `C_0` be the kernel of the inverse of `∂_u+h` on antiperiodic `L²([0,β];ℓ²)` (spin included if desired). Its time slices have norm at most one: at positive time separation the scalar kernel at eigenvalue `ε` is `exp(−(u-w)ε)/(1+exp(−βε))`, and negative separation follows by antiperiodicity. Both ordered limits at coincidence obey the bound. For any anchor, put `W=exp(a d)` with graph distance `d` to it and `a=(1+16βJ)^(-1)`. Either choice `W` or `W^(-1)` as conjugator changes `h` by a matrix `P` with row and column sums in absolute value bounded by `4J(e^a−1)≤8Ja≤1/(2β)`; the diagonal is unchanged. The fermionic-frequency inverse at `ω_k=(2k+1)π/β` has norm at most `1/|ω_k|` before conjugation, `2/|ω_k|` after, and the difference has norm at most `2||P||/ω_k²`. In time its difference series converges absolutely (the jump of the scalar identity cancels), so every conjugated slice has norm at most `1+β||P||/2≤5/4`, using `β^(−1)Σ_k ω_k^(−2)=β/4`. This scalar identity also follows from Parseval for the constant function on a half-open period in the antiperiodic Fourier basis. As an operator on time `L²`, the conjugated inverse has norm at most `2β/π≤β`.

The same conjugation gives `||W V(u) W^(-1)||≤4 e^a r≤3/(16β)`, with an even smaller bound for Holstein; the same holds with `W^(-1)`. This uses the degree-four Schur bound at each time. Thus `∂_u+h+V` is invertible by the norm-convergent resolvent identity

```
C = C_0 − C_0 V(1+C_0 V)^(-1) C_0.                     (5)
```

For the conjugated correction, the time endpoint maps into and out of time `L²` have norm at most `(5/4)√β` by the static slice bound; inserting the conjugated potential and inverse gives a uniform slice norm at most `(25/16)(3/16)/(1−3/16)=75/208`. Consequently each conjugated slice of `C` has norm at most `2`. These estimates include the ordered coincident limits by (5).

For completeness, on a finite one-particle space the Dyson evolution of a quadratic number-conserving Hamiltonian is the direct sum of exterior powers of the fundamental matrix `Y(u,w)` of `∂_u+h+V`. Its trace over electronic Fock space on one period is `det(1+Y(β,0))`. This determinant is nonzero if and only if the antiperiodic homogeneous equation has no solution. The normalized two-point time-ordered function is precisely (5): it obeys the homogeneous evolution away from the insertion, has the identity jump by the CAR, and is antiperiodic by cyclic trace. Wick's determinant formula holds for this possibly non-self-adjoint evolution. One proof is to use invertible exterior evolution between insertions to transport all fields to time zero (creation test vectors and the corresponding dual annihilation vectors transform contragrediently). For an ordered string the trace of the transported fields is taken against the exterior lift of `Y(β,0)`. For a diagonal matrix with `1+Y` invertible the occupation trace factors, giving Wick's rule by the CAR on independent modes. For diagonalizable `Y` use one-particle similarity and its invertible exterior lift, acting contragrediently on the fields; extend to all `Y` with `det(1+Y)≠0` by density and continuity of the normalized traces and their Wick polynomials. This verifies the formula with the prescribed order also at coincident insertions.

Two consequences will be used in the connected expansion.

* **Weighted attachments.** A centered row or column, with weight `exp(a d)` on the other endpoint, has `ℓ²` norm at most `2`. Cauchy–Schwarz yields an `ℓ¹` bound `2(1+2/a)` with weight `exp(a d/2)`: the square-sum of the remaining weights is `Σ exp(−a d)≤(1+2/a)²` by summing along each torus axis. If legs lie within one step of their vertex anchor, transferring the weight to anchor distance costs at most `e^a`; hence the weighted child-anchor sum, for either direction and fixed times and offsets, is at most `Q=64(1+4βJ)`. Indeed `1+2/a=3+32βJ≤8(1+4βJ)` and `e^a<3`. The same bound applies unweighted from any specified parent leg.
* **Wick minors.** For any interpolation with a positive semidefinite vertex matrix `s_ij` with diagonal one, tensor the one-particle space with unit vectors having this Gram matrix; evolve with `(h+V)⊗I` and put the corresponding label vector on each leg. Then its two-point contractions are the original ones times `s_ij`. For any minor with `k` creators and `k` annihilators, the static part has every subminor bounded by one: it is a normalized static thermal ordered Wick moment of unit-norm fields, bounded by trace Hölder splitting the imaginary-time intervals, with exponents `β/Δu`. Zero intervals use the operator norm. The correction in (5), **without spatial weights**, is a Hilbert-space Gram kernel with product of factor norms at most `β||V||_∞/(1−β||V||_∞)≤1/15`, since the two endpoint maps have norm at most `√β` and `β||V||_∞≤1/16`. For example factor the kernel into its endpoint maps on time `L²`, placing `−V(1+C_0 V)^(-1)` on one side, also tensored with identity. All correction subminors are therefore at most one by the wedge-product Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. Expanding a minor of `C_0+Δ` by the rows and columns assigned to `Δ` gives at most `Σ_j binom(k,j)²≤4^k` terms each of size at most one. Thus the claimed `4^k` bound holds also for the interpolated Wick minors. It does not rely on positivity of the time-dependent reference as an electronic Gibbs state.

## 4. Return the tails exactly; connected expansion

### Lemma 2: Exact independent local tail replacement

**PROVED.** For any free coordinate or history law `γ`, good event `G` with `0<γ(G)<1`, and bounded reference draw from `γ(·|G)`, independent Bernoulli replacement by a draw from `γ(·|Gᶜ)` with probability `γ(Gᶜ)` exactly restores the original law. If `γ(G)=1`, take no replacement and `W_ref=0`. For any finite collection of independent such laws and additive linear electronic couplings, its exact time-ordered integrand relative to the reference Wick functional is the product of `1+W_ref` defined below. If the pointwise coefficient envelope of `W_ref` has `128^m` norm at most `1/2`, it is also the exponential of an absolutely convergent labelled-even-vertex interaction with envelope norm at most twice that of `W_ref`. The assertion includes independent static and dynamic replacements on the same SSH bond; it does not require commuting bond operators. **Proof:** the next three paragraphs give the identity, envelope, and logarithm, respectively. The subsequent tree argument verifies convergence under the Wick functional in the theorem's regime.

Draw the reference histories independently from the free Gaussian dynamic law conditioned on `S≤r` (positive probability by (4) in the regime (1)). For each oscillator independently, with probability `P(S>r)` replace the reference draw `v_ref` by an independent draw from the complementary conditional law. The recovered law is exactly the original Gaussian law. Use the same separate replacement for SSH static coordinates outside `|b_0|≤R`. Holstein static coordinates are never truncated. Conditional on the bounded references this amounts in the time-ordered Dyson representation to factors `1+W_ref`, one per replacement label, where

```
W_ref = ∫_tail dγ(z) [ exp_Dyson(−∫_0^β [z(u)−ref(u)] B(u)du) − 1 ].
```

Here `γ` is the original unconditioned free law, `B` is the electronic density or bond bilinear in the reference interaction picture, and for static tails the coordinates in brackets are constant. In algebraic manipulations the exponential is the formal series of labelled even vertices: multiplication concatenates lists and the Wick functional applies time ordering to the full list. Thus even-vertex products commute at this stage, independently of operator commutators at distinct times. This proves the formula even when both replacements occur on the same SSH oscillator: the linear perturbations add before the full time ordering. The independent replacement is a reparametrization of the exact integral, not modification of its electron-weighted law.

Use the coefficient norm that multiplies the integrated absolute coefficient of a term with `2m` fermion legs by `128^m`, and sums over times and choices of bilinear monomials. A pointwise, reference-independent envelope of the coefficients exists: at any fixed choice and times in the time cube the bound before the factor `128^m` is `∫_tail (2S)^m/m! dγ` for the dynamic term, and the same with `|z|` for the static term. This follows because the reference size is no larger than the size of a tail draw. There are at most four choices at each bilinear occurrence, so the full norm of the dynamic envelope is at most (4); the static one is at most `2 exp(−β R²/(8U))`. For the latter, integrate `exp(1024β |z|)` against the static Gaussian on `|z|>R≥4096U`, use `1024|z|≤z²/(4U)`, and split the remaining exponent into equal parts to leave one integrable Gaussian with factor 2. This norm is submultiplicative, also for nonnegative pointwise envelopes at fixed labels. If the envelope norm is at most `1/2`, expand `log(1+W_ref)` as the ordinary series in this algebra, bounded coefficientwise by the majorant `Σ_(j≥1) W_env^j/j` of norm at most twice the tail norm. Its exponential is the exact tail replacement. Convergence of the conditional ordered products and these identities under the reference Wick functional follows from the `4^k` minor bound and the submultiplicative `128^m` weight, at any finite volume. This justification avoids treating the Dyson exponent as an equal-time interaction.

Let `w_m` be the sum/integral of the absolute coefficient envelopes of the resulting interaction vertices with `2m` legs at one anchor (two bond orientations for SSH). Every leg is within one of its anchor. In (1) the combined envelope satisfies

```
Σ_m w_m 128^m ≤ 64 exp(−g/2¹⁹) + 8 exp(−2²¹ x),
Q ≤ 2²¹ x,              x=1+βE≥1,                        (6)
```

where the second exponential may be omitted in Holstein. Indeed `β R²/(8U)≥2²¹x` in SSH. The orientation count is at most two for each kind of replacement; the `log` costs at most a factor two. Also `J≤4097(t+U+τ)` in SSH implies `1+4βJ≤2¹⁵x`, and Holstein is smaller. With `g≥2²⁵(1+log x)`, the dynamic part of `Q Σ w_m128^m` is at most `2²⁷ x exp(−64[1+log x])<1/64`; the static part is at most `2²⁴ x exp(−2²¹x)<1/64` (use `log x≤x` and `e>2`). This also verifies the preceding `1/2` assumptions. In particular the combined product is below `1/4` with room for sources.

Here are the convergence details. For a Wick moment of labelled even vertices, multiply each contraction between distinct vertices `i,j` by a common variable `s_ij`. Apply the polynomial forest formula: sum over forests, differentiate once in each edge variable, and integrate each forest edge over `[0,1]`, evaluating `s_ij` at the smallest edge on the path and zero between components (`s_ii=1`). This formula follows on each monomial by giving independent uniform weights to the labelled copies of its edges; the unique maximum spanning tree in each component is characterized by each off-tree weight lying below the smallest tree-path weight. Integrating over those off-tree weights gives exactly the forest integrals for the monomial, with the multiplicities of selected edges precisely the derivative factors. The interpolation matrix is positive semidefinite: it is the integral over thresholds of the partition matrices for edges above threshold. Forest terms factor over components, since whole even vertices commute under reordering. Thus the cumulant, or coefficient of the logarithm of the Wick partition, is exactly the sum over trees. Differentiating a tree edge chooses one unused leg at each end and leaves a single uncontracted Wick minor. For a vertex of valence `2m` a degree `d` costs at most `(2m)^d`; the minor bound is at most the product of `4^m` over the vertices.

The rooted majorant for the total weighted subtrees attachable to a specified parent leg, starting with height zero, is iteration from zero of

```
B ↦ Q Σ_(m≥1) 4^m (2m) w_m exp(2m B).
```

Indeed with fixed child template and times sum its anchor using the attachment bound; each descendant is bounded uniformly in the remaining parent legs and times. Partitioning labelled descendants among `k` unordered branches gives `1/k!` from the exponential generating series, and assigning their attachment legs costs at most `(2m B)^k`. Pointwise envelopes justify summing anchors before subsequent time integration. Since `2m≤2^m` and `e<3`, `4^m(2m) e^(2m)≤128^m`. Thus the interval `0≤B≤1` is invariant and its image is below `1/4`. A specified root of valence `2m` incurs only `4^m exp(2m B)` in addition to its own coefficient; unrooted connected sums are bounded per site by rooting and using `1/k!≤1/(k−1)!`. This gives absolute convergence. The finite-volume unconnected Wick series converges absolutely by the minor bounds; the absolutely summable connected series exponentiates to it by the forest decomposition (first compare Taylor coefficients under small common scaling and then sum at the physical coefficients, with strict margin). In particular the unconnected partition relative to the reference is nonzero, and the connected series gives its analytic logarithm for the small additional sources used below.

There is no probability assumption about interacting histories here. At zero source the exact recovered conditional partition `Z_ref(0)` is positive: each real full history contributes the square of the real determinant of a single spin, and the nonzero property follows from the expansion and the invertible bounded reference. The determinant square follows from exterior calculus as in §3 even for a full history at which the partition happens to vanish. No conditional reality at nonzero complex source is required in what follows.

## 5. Correlations and the fixed-number transfer

Apply the tree expansion with separate labelled even roots for the opposite charged fields. The normalized trace at fixed reference and with tails integrated is number preserving; each individual nonzero-charge expectation is zero. Thus the mixed log coefficient is precisely the two-point function. Equal times for disjoint supports can be treated in either order. For clarity, to bound the sum of its absolute coefficients over the second insertion's positions, add an arbitrarily small source amplitude for all translations of the second field and fix the first root. Use weights `exp(a d(anchor,anchor')/2)` on tree edges. At fixed source support radius the child-row sums remain finite: each ordinary or source offset costs at most `exp(a (R_s+1)/2)` or `exp(a R_s)`, for a fixed common source radius `R_s≥1`. A parent of source type may have a larger ordinary-child sum `C_s Q`; use the recursion rectangle `B_I≤1, B_S≤C_s` for some `C_s≥1` covering that extra weight. Without source children the image is at most `1/4,C_s/4` respectively. Source children contribute at most their amplitude times a finite weighted row sum times a fixed valence factor and `4^m exp(2m C_s)`; choose the amplitude small enough for invariance. The fixed first root is bounded by the same rule. Extracting the coefficient with exactly one second source and no further sources gives weighted summability over its anchor, since the product of edge weights on the unique tree path is at least the weight of the root separation. In particular the conditional absolute correlation is at most `c exp(−a d(0,y)/2)`, uniformly in reference, volume, and real chemical potential. The full grand-canonical bound follows by the positive conditional weights at zero source. A finite linear combination of field monomials changes only the prefactor.

For canonical transfer at a fixed displacement `y`, introduce a real Hamiltonian source `s` for the Hermitian real part of the translated product averaged by summing over anchor sites (do the imaginary part separately). The same expansion with **unweighted** attachments proves an analytic small-source disk of radius independent of volume and reference (allowed to depend on `y`). In the recursion each ordinary child can be summed from the actual parent leg with bound `Q`; for the new source type its finitely many offsets and fixed valence give finite child-row sums and a contribution proportional to `|s|β`. The rooted estimate thus bounds the per-volume absolute Taylor series at some small radius. The linear term is the conditional two-point function times the time length `β`, because differentiating the partition inserts the source at each time. The proof of the charged bound is uniform for insertions at any common time, even for nonstationary conditional references. Higher coefficients contribute at most `c_y |s|²` on a smaller disk. Per-volume log ratios to `Z_ref(0)` therefore have modulus at most `|s| c e^(−a d(0,y)/2)+c_y |s|²`. Exponentiate and average using `Z_ref(0)>0` and the triangle inequality. For the physical, positive GC partitions this gives the one-sided estimate, for either real sign,

```
V_sites^(-1) [log Z_GC(s)−log Z_GC(0)]
    ≤ |s| c e^(−a d(0,y)/2) + c_y |s|².                (7)
```

Here the source is number conserving, preserves translation symmetry, and is a finite-range Hamiltonian term at fixed `y`. The absolute estimate on the conditional log does not assume the conditional source partition is real.

We include the zero-source ensemble argument. Removing a bare link changes the normalized log partition by at most `2βt` by its operator norm. Removing an SSH coupling changes it by at most `2βU`: interpolate its coefficient by `s∈[0,1]`; Gaussian integration by parts gives for the magnitude of the derivative a bound `s∫du dv D(u,v)||B_e||²≤4sβU`. Explicitly the derivative inserts `−b_e(u) B_e(u)` and integration by parts in the Gaussian Dyson representation inserts `−s B_e(v)` with kernel `D`; the two-time function in the full, time-independent Gibbs trace is bounded by `||B_e||²≤4` by trace Hölder. This works in every number sector and at any size. The same argument applies to cutting a site coupling if needed, since `||n_x||≤2`. These bounds and the elementary free electronic sector dimensions bound the normalized log partition in every number sector by constants times the number of sites at fixed parameters.

Tile an `L×L` torus by open `k×k` blocks plus at most `O(kL)` sites in the leftover strip; cut block boundaries and isolate those sites at cost `O(V_sites/k)+O(kL)`. For decoupled blocks at fixed total number the partition sums products over allocations; its log differs from the maximum by at most the number of blocks times `log(2k²+1)` plus the analogous strip term. The strip contributes vanishing volume fraction at fixed `k`. The maximum per site tends, as block count grows, to the upper concave envelope of the block log partitions per site versus density. For the upper inequality use concavity and the mean block number. For the lower inequality take a convex combination attaining the envelope at the limiting density; approximate its fractions by integer block counts, and adjust the total number by changing at most `o(V_sites)` blocks for fixed `k` (an adjustment of `o(V_sites)` electrons, including the strip and rounding; a sector count can always move one unit toward the required total until it is reached). Sector log weights on a fixed block are bounded, so this costs `o(V_sites)`. The fixed-`k` envelope is continuous on `[0,2]` and the error after taking `L→∞` is at most `O(1/k)+log(2k²+1)/k²`.

More explicitly the second error term is `log(2k²+1)/k²`; both errors vanish with `k→∞` and are independent of the target density. Therefore the canonical limit for every density sequence exists and is concave, with a finite supporting slope at every interior density. The normalized GC log partition differs per site from the maximum of the canonical values plus `βμ N/V_sites` by at most `log(2V_sites+1)/V_sites`. Compactness of the density interval and the preceding convergence along arbitrary sequences give GC/canonical duality. This argument needs no uniqueness of density or Gibbs state. At an interior limiting density choose a supporting slope `−βμ` and set

```
δ_L = [log Z_GC(0;μ)−log Z_N(0)−βμ N]/V_sites → 0,
δ_L≥0.
```

For each real `s`, the number-sector inclusion gives `e^(βμN) Z_N(s)≤ Z_GC(s;μ)`. Convexity of the finite-volume log partition in the real source (by the positive Gibbs Duhamel quadratic form) bounds the normalized canonical derivative at zero between the two secants at `±s`, for `s>0`. By (7), its absolute value is at most `c e^(−a d(0,y)/2)+c_y s+δ_L/s`. Take the thermodynamic limit at fixed `s,y`, then send `s→0`, and only then increase separation. Translation symmetry and division by the factor `β` in the derivative identify the canonical correlation (real and imaginary parts separately). This proves §1 without any finite-volume equivalence for charged correlations.

Finally `τ³/[1+log(1+E/τ)]` is strictly increasing from zero to infinity; numerator increases and denominator strictly decreases. For `D=[(2/3)2²⁵U]^(1/3)`, `L=log(E/Ω)`, plug `c Ω^(2/3) L^(1/3)` with fixed `c>0` into (1). The factor `[1+log(1+E/τ)]/L` tends to `2/3` because `log L/L→0`; the other side divided by `U Ω² L` is `c³/U`. Bracket the root with `c<D` and `c>D` and then let `c→D`. Equation (2) and `τ_*=o(√Ω)` follow, with all logarithms dimensionless.

## 6. Independent route: pair-source pressure and an information obstruction

**PROVED, as a finite-volume source bound and an abstract obstruction only.** In the GC model let `A=Σ_x(η_x c†_(x↑) c†_(x↓)+conj(η_x)c_(x↓)c_(x↑))`, `|η_x|≤1`, and `p(h)=(τ/V_sites)log Z(H−h A)`. The phases can probe any momentum. At every real `h` the static and quantum pressures obey

```
0 ≤ p_qu(h)−p_st(h) ≤ ε,
ε = (M/V_sites) min{Ω/2, Ω²/(24τ)}.                      (8)
```

Here `M` is the number of oscillators and the comparison is before any differentiation. The source is anomalous, so this calculation is GC only. The proof uses two independent statements. For the sign, interpolate by coupling `sqrt(1−s)` times the original static Gaussian and `sqrt(s)` times an independent original quantum oscillator at each coordinate, keeping the zero Fourier mode fixed in law. Two Gaussian integrations by parts (one for each kind of variable upon differentiation) give the derivative of the log partition as `(1/2)Σ_{j,k≠0} [UΩ²/(ν_k²+Ω²)] Ĝ_j(ν_k)`, with `G_j` the full Gibbs time-ordered autocorrelation of the same bounded Hermitian `B_j`. At fixed static vector the remaining Hamiltonian is self-adjoint, even with the source. Pairing energy indices in its Lehmann expansion gives the real transform as a sum of `|B_{ab}|² (e^(−βE_a)−e^(−βE_b))(E_b−E_a)/[Z((E_b−E_a)²+ν_k²)]≥0`; equal energies contribute zero. The static-vector averaging has positive partition weights. Trace Hölder bounds the autocorrelation by `||B_j||²`, justifying the covariance sums and differentiation (or differentiate finite Gaussian Dyson expansions dominated as in §2). This proves the sign.

For the upper bound use a flat Brownian bridge with diffusion `UΩ²` for each quantum coordinate, and include the spring term in the matrix potential `V(b)`. The diagonal free kernel factor is `(2πβ UΩ²)^(-M/2)`. For equal time slices along the bridge, Schatten Hölder bounds the absolute matrix trace of the product of `n` terms by the geometric mean of their `Tr exp(−βV(b_k))`, hence by their arithmetic mean. Integrate over the initial coordinate and use translation invariance in each slice; the trace is bounded by the free kernel factor times the integral of `Tr exp(−β V(b))`. The harmonic lower form bound (complete the square with bounded electronic coupling) and Gaussian integration of the right-hand side justify the Brownian trace formula and passage from time slices. The free harmonic trace is `1/[2sinh(βΩ/2)]` per oscillator and the static Gaussian normalization is `sqrt(2π U/β)`, giving `Z_qu/Z_st≤[sinh(βΩ/2)/(βΩ/2)]^M`. For `x≥0`, `sinh x/x≤e^x` by integration of `cosh(sx)`, and `sinh x/x≤e^(x²/6)` by `(2n+1)!≥6^n n!`. This proves (8), including its units of energy per site.

At fixed static coordinates, exchange creation and annihilation in the down-spin orbitals (a finite CAR change of variables). The source is then a number-conserving bilinear `dΓ(M_η)` with Hermitian off-diagonal one-body matrix of squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm `2Σ|η_x|²≤2V_sites`; the rest of the conditional Hamiltonian remains quadratic and number conserving in these variables, up to a constant. In its diagonal occupation basis the variance of the source is `Σ_{ij}|(M_η)_{ij}|² f_i(1−f_j)≤2V_sites`. The canonical Duhamel average here refers to the thermal inner product, not a fixed-particle ensemble: its connected value is bounded by the ordinary variance by the logarithmic mean of two Boltzmann weights being at most their arithmetic mean (integrate the weighted geometric mean). Thus the conditional pressure curvature is at most `2β`. The zero-source first derivative vanishes by electron number symmetry in the original variables; integrating the conditional bound and averaging with its own zero-source partition weights yields

```
0≤p_st(h)−p_st(0)≤β h²,
0≤p_qu(h)−p_qu(0)≤β h²+ε.                                 (9)
```

The lower inequalities follow from evenness (electron phase rotation by `π/2`) and convexity. Equations (8)–(9) operate on the full Gibbs trace, probe a bulk susceptibility rather than winding, and do not depend on any static-reference derivative or absolute tree envelope. But the additive `ε` in (9) cannot be differentiated twice. The missing estimate for this route is a uniform upper quadratic bound near `h=0` for `[p_qu(h)−p_st(h)]−[p_qu(0)−p_st(0)]` (or a direct bound on the corresponding susceptibility).

Indeed (8), even with globally analytic, even, convex, uniformly Lipschitz pressures and bounded reference susceptibility, gives no finite upper bound on the zero-source curvature. In dimensionless variables put `f(h)=sqrt(1+h²)−1`, `q_δ(h)=a(sqrt(δ²+h²)−δ)` with `a=min(1/3,sqrt(2ε/3))>0`, `δ>0`, and `κ=ε/2`. Take `p_st=f`, `p_qu=κ log(exp(f/κ)+exp(q_δ/κ))`. For `|h|≤1`, `f(h)≥h²/3`; for `|h|≥1`, `f(h)≥|h|/3`, as the ratio `|h|/(sqrt(1+h²)+1)` is increasing. Since `q_δ≤a|h|`, the positive part of `q_δ−f` is at most `3a²/4≤ε/2`. Log-sum-exp adds at most `κ log 2<ε/2` to the maximum, so (8) holds for all `h`. Both functions are even and real analytic; convexity follows from convexity of `f,q_δ` and the positive variance term in the second derivative of the log-sum; their slopes have modulus at most one. Yet `p_qu''(0)=(1+a/δ)/2` is unbounded as `δ→0`, whereas `p_st''(0)=1`. These are test functions, not realizations of either electron–phonon model. The abstract obstruction is for the source comparison alone and is distinct from the parent harmonic-flux obstruction.
